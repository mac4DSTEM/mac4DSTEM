# Visual acceptance — Track B

**The human half of verification.** Adopted 2026-08-17
([`docs/v2-scope.md`](v2-scope.md) §6.2–6.3) when the XCUITest QC playthrough
was retired as the acceptance test.

This is not a fallback for missing automation. It catches a **disjoint class of
defect**, and it has out-performed the full automated suite twice:

- **2026-08-06**, tag day, all 30 harnesses green — ten minutes of driving the
  app by hand produced two real defects: a colormap control absent from the only
  workspace that needed it, and a readiness row contradicting its own detail
  line.
- **2026-08-14**, first clean-account run, roughly fifteen minutes — three more,
  none reachable by any harness in the repo.

It is also the only thing that can answer *"are these detected disks **right**?"*,
which is a judgement, not a measurement.

---

## When to run it

**Any change to what the app draws, or to where a control lives.** Also before
any tag, and after any macOS version bump.

Not needed for: numeric-only changes with a `tools/` fixture, doc changes, or
build/packaging work that does not alter a screen.

---

## How it works

1. The assistant writes a **short, specific** checklist for the change at hand —
   what to open, what to expect, and what would count as wrong. Specific: *"the
   Result workspace colormap picker is present and changes the map"*, not
   *"check the Results screen"*.
2. The release owner opens the real app, drives it once, and sends screenshots.
3. Anything that looks wrong becomes an item in
   [`docs/open-items.md`](open-items.md) — **not** an app-code change made to
   satisfy the checklist. The eval-only rule that governed the QC playthrough
   carries over unchanged: *if the app blocks the pipeline, that is a finding.*

Ten to fifteen minutes of human time. Budget it as part of "done", the way doc
updates are.

**A method note that changes what the assistant can drive.** System Events'
`click at` is ELEMENT-based — it resolves an accessibility element and presses
it — and SwiftUI panes backed by `NSViewRepresentable` expose no element, so
clicks on them silently do nothing. That briefly looked like an app regression
and was not one. Real `CGEvent` clicks and drags work, via a small scratch tool
built for this sitting. **Drag-dependent rows are therefore no longer
owner-only**, which is what made F1.3c's re-run possible.

**And the complement, found 2026-09-01 — an agent needs BOTH mechanisms.**
`CGEvent` clicks drive buttons, but they do **not** toggle SwiftUI
`DisclosureGroup` rows: the sidebar is an `AXOutline` and its rows resolve as
`row N of outline 1`, where System Events' element click opens them immediately
and a real click does nothing. Verified with a control — the *Display*
disclosure refused `CGEvent` too, and nothing is wrong with it. So: **element
click for disclosures and ordinary controls, `CGEvent` for panes and drags.**
Four failed clicks on one disclosure is not a hit-target defect.

## Recording a run

Put the outcome in [`docs/open-items.md`](open-items.md) — the date, the
dataset, what was driven, and every finding. Screenshots live wherever the
release owner keeps them; the repo does not need them, but the **findings**
must not live only in a chat log.

Until an automated visual baseline exists, any claim that a UI change "looks
right" rests on someone having looked. Say who, and when.

### The free-form pass — a recognised mode, added 2026-08-31

The owner asked to drive his own datasets rather than only the scripted rows.
That is **an addition with a reorder, not a substitution**, and both halves of
that sentence are load-bearing.

**Why it is an addition.** A checklist can only find what someone already
suspected; that is its structural ceiling. This repo's own record is the
argument: on tag day all 30 harnesses were green and ten minutes of hand-driving
produced two defects; the first clean-account run produced three more. Neither
set came from a row. Free-form is also the *only* route to sitting 3 — a
multi-GB cube is a standing data block, and the owner's own cubes are the
missing resource.

**Why it is not a substitution.** Five of the fourteen done criteria in
`docs/v2-release.md` §5 are worded as dependencies on specific rows, and two —
DC2 ("the F1 queue is empty") and DC13 ("full Track B standing pass A–F
recorded") — cannot be discharged by an unstructured session, because there is
nothing to tick. Free-form is strong at *is this ugly, broken or stuck* and weak
at *is this **wrong***, which is the half this app's contract is about: §C exists
because a run that finds only the central beam still produces a number and a
green step. On unfamiliar data there is no analytic answer to check against.
Three classes it cannot reach at all: **absence checks** (F1.3, F1.14's section
that must be GONE, F1.31's refusal that must not appear — you cannot notice the
absence of a thing you were not told to look for), **deliberate-failure checks**
(F1.15, F1.23 — nobody sabotages their own analysis casually), and **constructed
fixtures** (F1.26's impossible sidecar, F1.42's simple-cubic-on-gold demo).

**The order is not stylistic.** Drive free-form **first, unprimed**, then read
the rows. F1.37 and F1.40 ask whether something *reads as broken* or *reads as a
warning*; being told the answer first destroys the only measurement those rows
wanted.

**What to capture, so a finding is evidence and not anecdote.** This list is
derived from the three recorded cases where a Track B observation could not be
scored — F1.3b (scored PASSED on a dialog appearing while both panes were
blank), F1.17 (unscoreable from cropped screenshots for two days), F1.27 (still
unscored, because one screenshot cannot say whether Change… was used or the
panel was cancelled).

1. **The build.** ⌘R from Xcode, never `open -a mac4DSTEM` — that launches the
   installed release, and the cost of that mistake is on the record (2026-08-19).
   Paste `git rev-parse --short HEAD` once at the start of the sitting.
2. **The dataset, by full path**, plus shape (Ry×Rx×Qy×Qx), file size and
   format. "One of my cubes" is unusable: F1.26 and F1.45 both turn on
   properties of a specific file.
3. **The exact click path**, in order, including anything changed off defaults.
   F1.45 is unreachable at shipped defaults — a report that omits "I raised Min
   relative intensity" cannot be reproduced.
4. **A full-window screenshot, not a crop.** Geometry is the evidence for every
   clipping and collision row.
5. **Strings verbatim** — the status line, and any error. Copy the whole line
   after "HDF5 reported:", and copy the **errno**.
6. **Expected, then observed, in that order, in your own words.** "I thought X
   would happen" is the half that makes an unanticipated finding legible later,
   and the half a free-form session naturally drops.
7. **The display settings — histogram range, gamma, log/linear, colormap.**
   Added 2026-09-01 after they invalidated a live inference: an observation about
   rings appearing in a Bragg vector map was attributed to a detection-threshold
   change when the histogram range and gamma had *also* been adjusted. These are
   **ordinary user actions that change what is visible without changing the
   data**, so a screenshot without them cannot be reasoned about. If you adjust
   contrast to see something, say so — that is not a confession, it is the
   measurement condition.

And **one negative result per sitting**: *"opened N cubes, drove these
workspaces, found nothing"* — dated, in `docs/open-items.md`. A pass with no
findings is a claim, and it needs the same record as a failure.

**Three standing cautions.**

- **Do not copy your own cubes into `References/training_dataset/`.**
  `tools/real-data-acceptance/run.sh` and the campaign runner both glob `*.h5`
  there, so anything dropped in silently joins the aggregate gate and changes
  what green means (`docs/development-process.md` §8). Bulk data lives outside;
  harnesses take explicit paths.
- **Do not drive the app while `tools/run-tests.sh unit` is running** — one
  defaults domain, two drivers; it produced a spurious
  `SidebarDensityMeasurementTests` failure on 2026-08-31.
- **The eval-only rule.** If the app blocks your science, that is a finding for
  `docs/open-items.md`, not a change to make it go. This is the discipline most
  at risk when the driver is doing real work rather than scoring rows.

**One navigation fact that will otherwise cost you a sitting.** Once a dataset
is loaded, **"Open with Options…", the Recents list and "Try Demo Data" are all
unreachable in that window** — all three live only on the welcome screen
(`UI/ProductWorkspaceViews.swift:44-66`), none has a File-menu item or a
shortcut, and the sidebar's open control is inside `if !appState.hasDataset`
(`UI/ContentView.swift:122`). Plain ⌘O survives, so the *configured* open is the
one that disappears after first use. **The escape is ⌘N for a second window.**
Every configurator row — F1.8, F1.19, F1.30, F1.31 — needs that. (Filed as a UX
finding for S22; recorded here because it changes how the rows are driven, not
because it is being fixed now.)
